INTRODUCTION

Making and developing video games is not easy, but it has never been easier than it was before. Back in the 1990s and 2000s developers had to squeeze their games onto all kinds of underpowered systems, consoles and handheld devices. Modern day developers don't have to deal with these extreme restrictions with home computers, consoles and even handheld devices being more powerful than ever. So why do we sometimes get diminishing returns?

The game boy family is a perfect example of a system that really lacks power. But that didnt stop developers from SQUEEZING anything onto them!

Recently on the internet I've seen discussions online when comparing newer games to the older titles and the conclusion is most of the time that the most recent ones look and perform underwhelmingly. Meanwhile the old ones do hold up visually and perform better while having way less requirements.

A little sneek peak into a development studio.

So how about we take a look at some? Before we start this tour I would like to mention that this is a bit of a subjective list. I'm just a 16 year old college student so I can't include all of the titles that are technically mindblowing but I tried to include as many of them as I could. So without any further ado…

ALIEN RESURRECTION (THE GAME)

Released for: Playstation 1.

A picture of the player blasting an alien with a shotgun.

No, I'm not talking about the terrible movie from 1997. I'm talking about the tie-in game that was released 3 years later on the PS1 in 2000. Just two weeks before the PS2 would release in North America. Yeah it's not the most impressive game from the year, but for the hardware it was on it was incredible.

The way the levels and stages are lit is impressive, the lighting system they used is something you would have seen on a PS2 game. The atmosphere is top notch, and makes the boring ship from the movie more scary and rundown. When the game boots up there's even a cool image of a shadowy silhouette of an alien, with a little paragraph of text which tells you to play it in the dark.

A picture of a dark area while the player has a flamethrower.

So they clearly cared about the look and feel of the game and wanted to show it off. The aliens and weapon models look great too, it might not seem like it considering how blocky and chunky the game looks but for the limitations of the hardware it's commendable.

So even if you have a PS1 or know how to emulate it, why shouldn't you really play it? Simple, this game is brutally, brutally hard. Ammo and health supplies are harder to find than a home in the USA that you can actually own and don't have to rent. Meaning you have to play like a tryhard and try to consistently hit the fast enemies you come across. Aliens can also randomly appear throughout the levels, which is even more bad news because you WILL not have any spare ammo on you. Hopefully this issue will be fixed when someone will eventually remaster the game, hopefully.

JURASSIC PARK OPERATION GENESYS

Released for: Microsoft Windows, Playstation 2 and Xbox.

Some lovely cover art for the game.

The new Jurassic evolution trilogy developed by Frontier are undeniable hitters, giving players the ability to build and manage a dinosaur theme park. They look incredible and earn the system requirements they ask for, and the only reason I'm not talking about them is that all of them have been released fairly recently. Those games are also described as a spiritual successor to Jurassic park operation genesys or JPOG for short, the game I'm going to talk about here.

Like the evolution games you run a dino zoo, however instead of a chunk of land you get an entire island to build your park on. Said island is of course the fictional island Isla Nubliar, which is heavily based of Hawaii. (There's a Google maps picture at the bottom.) Unlike evolution this game has a grid system you build on, which might be a little restrictive but you do have a lot of land to work with. When it comes to dinosaurs there are 25 different species that have similar or different requirements.

The herbivores need trees and the carnivores require goat feeders. The dinosaurs do and will take up quite the space, especially the gentle giants. But you can work around that by making different species of herbivores share enclosures together. Some herbivores are completely fine with this but not all, and carnivores can't really share a room with any other species.

The coolest thing about the game and this system is that there's a site b mode where you manage an island without any tourists, just the dinos. If you keep them comfortable they will just casually mind their own business in peace. Even carnivores will remain calm and won't start trouble. It's such a cute and fun mode that's unfortunately exclusive to this game. None of the evolution games have this and I'm pretty baffled by that.

Visually it's pretty strong, the dinosaur models and the environment are really solid for their time. Compared to other typhoon games like "Symcity" or "Zoo tycoon 2", it does allow you to build a lot of things, and won't suffer from that which is doubly impressive considering how good the game looks.

Aside from having no site b mode, the evolution games are probably the better option in terms of building your dream park. But if you have PS2, Xbox or know where to emulate the game on pc I strongly recommend you give JPOG a whirl.

DOOM 3

Released for: Microsoft Windows and Xbox.

Re-released in 2012 for: Playstation 3 and Xbox 360.

Re-released in 2019 for: Playstation 4, Xbox one and Nintendo switch.

The poster has a hellknight on it, a big, dangerous enemy you will encounter later in the game.

To be honest, I could have included any of the titles from this series. All of them are impressive and manage to push the first person shooter formula to its limits, and DOOM 3 is no different. It was released in 2004 and was developed by ID software who are no strangers to making technological powerhouses.

It ran on the team's homemade game engine, the ID TECH 4 which allowed the game to have revolutionary shadows and dynamic lighting that the levels are stuffed with. While the graphics haven't aged perfectly, for the time they were jaw dropping and the environments and levels were impressive.

Unfortunately the double barreled shotgun is only available in the two expansions for the game: "Resurrection of evil" and "the lost mission".

The space station on mars you wander through is rundown and falling apart. The monsters are terrifying and detailed, but the humans you might encounter look off and uncanny. They haven't aged as gracefully as everything else. One thing that has aged genuinely perfectly are the dark shadows and the way light itself travels.

You have a flashlight that you can't hold at the same time while carrying a gun, instead you have to switch back to a weapon to defend yourself. That might sound like a minor thing but the space station is shrouded in darkness, so you will sometimes have to switch to the flashlight to see where you're going. Enemies like the imp, cacodemon and hellknight can toss gloving projectiles at you, which slightly illuminates the space they travel. Some enemies have sinister glowing eyes which give away their position.

This picture of the player holding the chaingun is taken from the 2012 re-release, the bfg edition.

Basically, DOOM 3 is built with darkness in mind, the impressive dark shadows have gameplay purposes other than to look impressive and that's awesome. If you want to play DOOM 3 I recommend the original version over the re-releases. In that version you're given WAY too much ammo, the game is much brighter and your flashlight is now shouldermounted. All of those things impact the incredible atmosphere a little in a bad way, and the amount of ammo this version showers you with is just ridiculous. But if it's your only way to play it can be serviceable.

BATMAN ARKHAM KNIGHT

Released for: Microsoft Windows, Playstation 4 and Xbox one.

Re-released in 2023 for: Nintendo switch.

A gourgeous picture of Batman gliding in the city.

Batman Arkham Knight is a masterpiece, especially in the tech and graphical department. This monstrosity looks like it could have been released this year. It's that incredible.

This game and the arkham series it comes from of course puts you in the shoes of the batman himself, and has the best incarnation. Throughout all the games you switch between “beat em up” “sections and metal gear solid” like stealth sections. It's the same in Knight, but now there's an extra gameplay layer, the Batmobile. It can turn into a tank and tear through the city at aggressive speed while you drive it. While the open world isn't the biggest it is pretty densely designed, and the game is very good at loading or baking stuff in quickly while you glide or drive through the map.

Another gourgeous picture, this time of batman jumping into the Batmobile.

This game does have a realistic artstyle like most games today do, but there's a proper gritty and ominous tone and feel to it. The city you travel around is moody and mechanical. The billboards and cars have a classic 50s and 60s vibe and style. To put it simply, the game has a dang good and focused art direction.

What makes Batman Arkham Knight truly special is the engine it uses, a heavily modified unreal engine 3.5. It was popular in the late 2000s and early 2010s but around 2015 it was getting outdated. Even in its prime the games it powered looked “dry”, awkward character models. Yet Arkham Knight doesn't have any of these problems. If you ask me, this is the most impressive superhero game ever made, and I wouldn't be surprised if it's the last one.

If you're a fan of action games or you are a Batman fan, I HIGHLY recommend this one, even if it's only 30 frames per second on PS4 or Xbox one. I can't say the same for the Nintendo switch version, that one is a malfunctioning mess. But on any other system… LOOK NO FURTHER.
